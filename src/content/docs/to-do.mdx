Harbor separates **expiration dates** from **tasks**. A passport has an expiration date stored on its document. Renewing that passport is a task you complete. A bill can have a payment task with a due date and amount.

## Where to-dos come from

From the Inbox card's ticked clause, or you add them by hand. Each has a kind, pay, file, renew, fetch or review, a due date, and for payments an amount. One document can carry two: a tax assessment says pay by the 14th and object by the 28th. A to-do can also have no document at all, such as "ask the building manager about the meter reading", and tasks without a due date appear under *No date*.

## The list

The **To do** page groups them: Overdue, Today, This week, Later, No date, with Done kept below. Home shows the same things under *Needs attention*, beside upcoming expiries; only the to-dos get a checkbox.

Three rules run through every place they appear:

- **Overdue is the only red.** A date that has not passed is amber at most. Upcoming tasks are not marked overdue.
- **The badge counts overdue and due today, and nothing else.** Future tasks remain in the list without increasing the badge count.
- **Nothing is deleted when it is done.** Closing keeps the row, who closed it, and when, so you can check when someone marked it complete. This records the action in Harbor; it does not verify a bank payment. *Dismissed* is a third state for the obligation that was never yours.

## Repeating

A repeating to-do, monthly, quarterly, yearly or every two years, creates its successor when you close it, dated from the occurrence you closed rather than from today, so a bill paid three days late does not drag the series later. Dismissing ends the series.

## What Harbor does not do

Reminders stay in the app. There is no email digest or calendar feed in this version, and Harbor never marks a bill paid on its own, because it cannot see the bank.
