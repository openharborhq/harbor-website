Keep a printed recovery sheet separate from the server. Recovering from an encrypted backup requires both the backup and the correct keys; Harbor cannot replace lost keys for you. Anyone with this information and access to your backup may be able to recover your records.

## What goes on it

Use `harbor break-glass` to display the recovery information. Review the output and complete the following checklist before storing the sheet:

1. **The passphrase** that unlocks the data volume at boot.
2. **The master key**, which decrypts every document and every sealed secret, including the authenticator secrets and mail passwords: `cat /data/secrets/kek`
3. **The backup password**, which decrypts the snapshots: `cat /data/secrets/restic-password`
4. **Where the backups are**, and the credential that reaches them: the `RESTIC_REPOSITORY` line and the B2 key, the SFTP login, or which disk.
5. **Your account recovery codes**, saved during first run, if your authenticator becomes unavailable. Keep these with the sheet; do not assume the command includes them.
6. **The address of [Restore from a backup](/docs/restore)** and the Harbor release you are running.

## Where it goes

In a safe, or with someone you trust in another house. Not on the box, and not in the vault.

## When to reprint it

Whenever any line changes: a new backup target, a rotated key, a new volume after a restore. Replace outdated copies so the information matches your current setup.
